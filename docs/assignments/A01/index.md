Task B: Product Analysis
Product: Motorcycle Tire Lever

The product I selected is a motorcycle tire lever, specifically a hand-operated lever used to remove or install a tire bead from a wheel rim. This product is directly related to motorcycle maintenance and racing because tire removal and installation are common maintenance operations for dirt bikes and race motorcycles. The reason I picked this product is I had a very annoying time replacing my dirtbike tire this past weekend.

The tire lever is primarily mechanical because it transfers a manually applied force through its geometry to generate a larger force at the tire bead. The product consists of one primary component, making it suitable for the three-component limit established by the assignment.

Primary Function

The primary function of the motorcycle tire lever is to multiply an applied hand force and transfer that force through a controlled contact point to separate or install a tire bead relative to a wheel rim.

Rather than simply describing the tool as something used to change a tire, the engineering function is the controlled application of mechanical advantage to overcome the resistance between the tire bead and rim.

Governing Mechanical Model

The primary governing model is the moment equation:

M = Fd

where:

M = applied moment about the contact point
F = force applied by the user
d = perpendicular distance between the applied force and the pivot point

The tire lever functions as a lever. During tire removal, the hook engages the tire bead while a portion of the lever contacts the wheel rim. The rim contact acts as the reaction point, allowing the user's applied force to create a moment that lifts the tire bead over the rim.

A more complete static model can be represented as:

ΣM = 0

For the lever to move the tire bead, the moment produced by the user's input force must exceed the resisting moment produced by the tire bead and its contact with the rim.

One important assumption is that the tire lever and wheel rim can be treated as rigid bodies during the operation. This assumption allows the lever to be analyzed using rigid-body statics rather than accounting for significant deformation of the lever or rim. A second practical assumption is that the lever does not slip at its contact point with the rim while force is being applied.

The effectiveness of the lever therefore depends on both the applied force and the geometry of the tool. Increasing the distance between the user's hand and the pivot point increases the available moment for the same input force.

The tire lever is a single-piece mechanical component with several functional geometric features. The elongated body provides the moment arm between the user's hand and the rim contact point. Increasing this distance increases the moment generated by a given input force according to M = Fd.

The removal end contains a hook that is inserted between the tire bead and the rim. The hook geometry allows the tool to capture the tire bead while maintaining contact with the rim. A notch near the removal hook provides a pivoting contact with the rim, allowing the tire lever to rotate while the hook lifts the tire bead.

The opposite end contains an installation hook. This geometry allows the same tool to assist with installation rather than requiring a separate tool. The installation portion engages the rim while the curved surface contacts the tire. The curved geometry helps the lever move around the rim while reducing the likelihood of damaging the tire.

The handle section also includes a finger opening and guard. These features position the user's hand relative to the lever and reduce the possibility of the user's fingers contacting the spokes during operation.

The patent specifically describes a unitary tire lever containing a removal hook, installation hook, finger opening, finger guard, and thumb-contact surfaces. It also describes smooth curved surfaces intended to allow the tool to slide around the rim while reducing tire damage.

The tire lever design I researched is U.S. Patent Application Publication US2311789A, titled Tire Lever. The patent lists Ivan M Taylor as the inventor and individual as the assignee. The application was filed on April 13, 1932, published on Febuary 23, 1943.

The patent describes a tire lever designed to remove and install tires without requiring the lever to attach to the wheel hub axle. The design uses separate removal and installation hooks located on opposite sides of the tool. The patent explains that the removal hook uses a notch as a pivot against the rim while the hook engages the tire bead.

One significant design decision in the patented tire lever is the use of separate removal and installation hooks on opposite sides of the same tool. The patent explains that the removal hook is shaped to pivot against the rim and engage the tire bead, while the installation hook engages the rim and provides a smooth curved surface against the tire.

## Decide
Homepage Identity

Professors, engineers, recruiters, and other technical readers will be able to access an engineering record through the homepage. Rather than offering an extensive personal biography, its main objective is to convey the contents of the portfolio and set the documentation standard followed throughout the semester. As a result, the homepage will specify the goal of the portfolio, arrange assignments in a logical order, and provide access to each assignment via the navigation menu. To make it easy for a technical reader to find calculations, assumptions, design decisions, and supporting documentation, each assignment will follow a standard format based on analysis, engineering choices, and communication.

Intentional Customization

I'm going to switch the color scheme of the portfolio to a dark gray and white with a blue bits. This aims to enhance readability and provide a visual connection to automotive and motorsports engineering. The altered color scheme offers visual differentiation while preserving adequate contrast between the background and technical information because the default color scheme, even though similar to school colors, does not explicitly convey the engineering focus of the portfolio. Therefore, rather than altering the design out of personal taste, the modification is based on the functional requirement of assisting a reader in distinguishing navigation elements, headings, and technical information.

Documentation Standard

Every assignment entry will state the governing model and assumptions, show the evidence used to make each engineering decision, and provide enough dimensions, calculations, and reasoning that another engineering student could reproduce the result without asking me for additional information.
